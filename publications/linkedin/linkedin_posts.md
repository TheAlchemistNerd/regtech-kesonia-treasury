# Revised RegTech–KESONIA launch posts

**28 September 2026. Draft copy for editorial use; not posted externally.**

**Part 1**

KESONIA pricing starts with a benchmark, but the lending decision depends on dated cash. The borrower has a payment calendar; the lender has expected collections; Treasury has funding obligations. Part 1 of the revised series connects those records and explains the Kenyan framework's scope, the customer premium and the responsibilities of contracts, risk services, accounting and reporting. It also distinguishes financing a business from financing a ring-fenced receivable stream. The companion examples show what can be reproduced and where institutional evidence is still needed.

**Part 2**

A compounding formula becomes useful when every fixing and day weight can be explained. Part 2 works through interval boundaries, weekends, holidays, publication timing, lookback and index verification, then builds a synthetic curve from instrument quotations and checks that those instruments reprice. The worked examples separate historical accrual, projected cash and discounting. The code is a research reference with stated conventions and tests, not a claim that every institution's SQL engine or contract is already covered.

**Part 3**

A default score does not explain the full price of a loan. Part 3 derives the customer margin from expected monetary income, funding, servicing, credit loss and a capital hurdle. A worked example reconciles the rate, RAROC and economic profit. Dated funding, deposit behaviour, recovery timing and borrower affordability then show why the same quoted spread can produce different outcomes. Accounting follows the actual event: refreshing risk, changing contractual terms and recognising a journal remain separate decisions.

**Part 4**

A calculation has not completed its job when a dashboard turns green. Part 4 follows approved results through posting, acknowledgement and reconciliation, and follows a treasury proposal through confirmation, booking and settlement. Duplicate messages, corrected rates and missing acknowledgements are part of the design. The software map distinguishes analytical, transaction and accounting roles across Oracle, SAP, Microsoft, MORS and Abrigo. The local demonstration is separated from the later work needed to prove a live institutional interface.

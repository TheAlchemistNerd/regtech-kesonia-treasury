1. Expected Loss (EL): The "Average Day" CostExpected Loss represents the predictable, statistical average amount of money a bank anticipates losing over a standard one-year horizon. Because it is considered a recurring operational expense, it must be priced directly into the loan's interest rate margin and offset via accounting provisions (under IFRS 9 / ECL). riskhub.org +2The Mathematical Equation: LinkedIn·Gabriel Kariuki +1\[\text{Expected\ Loss\ (EL)}=\text{PD}\times \text{LGD}\times \text{EAD}\]Probability of Default (PD %): The statistical likelihood that a specific SME or borrower will default within 12 months. Corporate Finance InstituteLoss Given Default (LGD %): The economic percentage of the net facility size that will be lost irreversibly after exhausts on collateral liquidations and debt collection costs. Corporate Finance InstituteExposure at Default (EAD $): The gross outstanding dollar amount on the credit facility at the exact moment of default (inclusive of unused revolving limits drawn down frantically by a struggling borrower). riskhub.org🗒 Practical SME Loan Example: An agricultural machinery SME takes a Sh5,000,000 term loan. The bank assigns a PD of 4% based on seasonal sector volatility, and an LGD of 40% because it is secured by the equipment.\[\text{EL}=0.04\times 0.40\times \text{Sh5,000,000}=\text{Sh80,000}\]Pricing Rule: The bank must charge an explicit credit premium of at least 1.6% (Sh80,000 / Sh5,000,000) in the interest margin just to cover baseline defaults. riskhub.org2. Economic Capital (EC): The "Doomsday" Volatility ShieldEconomic Capital represents the buffer required to absorb Unexpected Loss (UL). Unexpected losses are volatile, severe spikes in defaults that occur during systemic recessions or localized market shocks. ElysianNXT +2Unlike regulatory capital (which uses rigid formulas prescribed by external Basel rules), Economic Capital is an internal, risk-sensitive metric. It calculates the amount of equity capital required to keep the bank solvent at a specific target confidence level (e.g., 99.9% Value at Risk, aligned to maintain a chosen corporate credit rating). Learnsignal +3The Mathematical Equation: Learnsignal\[\text{Economic\ Capital\ (EC)}=\text{Value\ at\ Risk\ (VaR)}_{\alpha }-\text{Expected\ Loss\ (EL)}\]\[\text{VaR}_{\alpha }\] (Credit Value at Risk): The worst-case loss threshold on the portfolio over a 1-year holding period, given a target confidence level (α). For an AA bank rating, this is typically calibrated to 99.95%. Investopedia +1Subtracting EL: Because EL is already covered by loan pricing margins and regular income statements, the capital buffer only needs to cover the unpredicted deviation extending into the tail of the distribution. Learnsignal +1🗒 Practical Portfolio View: If a bank evaluates an uncollateralized micro-SME trade portfolio totaling Sh100,000,000: Expected Losses are historical and model-mapped at Sh2,500,000 (2.5%).A 1-year portfolio stress model simulation shows that under a major economic shock (99.9% confidence), total losses could surge to Sh12,000,000 (Credit VaR).Economic Capital Required = Sh12,000,000 - Sh2,500,000 = Sh9,500,000. LinkedIn·Gabriel Kariuki +1📊 Direct Comparison: EL vs. EC in Risk-Adjusted PricingFeatureExpected Loss (EL)Economic Capital (EC)Concept DefinitionThe anticipated "mean" loss from normal operations.The cushion required to absorb severe, random tail risk events.Statistical TargetMean / Expected value of the loss distribution.High-percentile tail cut-off (e.g., 99.9% or 99.95% VaR).Impact on the P&LDirectly treated as an operating expense or explicit cash charge.Imposes an opportunity cost (the equity hurdle rate must be earned on it).RAROC Pricing RolePlaced in the Numerator (subtracted from gross revenues).Placed in the Denominator (the total risk capital at risk).Financial MitigationCovered by loan margin add-ons & general credit provisioning.Covered by retained equity capital and tier-1 reserves.📈 Linking Both via the RAROC EquationWhen pricing an SME credit line, the bank aggregates both calculations to evaluate capital efficiency. def calculate_raroc(lgd, ec_rate):
    gross_revenue = facility_amount * interest_rate
    funding_costs = facility_amount * cost_of_funds_rate
    expected_loss = facility_amount * pd * lgd
    net_income = gross_revenue - funding_costs - operating_costs - expected_loss
    economic_capital = facility_amount * ec_rate
    raroc = net_income / economic_capital
    return net_income, economic_capital, raroc

It implements the standard Drawdown Method: \[\text{Empirical\ CCF}=\frac{\text{Balance\ at\ Default}-\text{Balance\ 12\ Months\ Prior}}{\text{Total\ Limit}-\text{Balance\ 12\ Months\ Prior}}\]pythonimport numpy as np
import pandas as pd

# 1. Generate historical default records for an SME revolving portfolio
np.random.seed(42)
num_samples = 1000

sme_data = pd.DataFrame({
    'sme_id': range(1, num_samples + 1),
    'sector': np.random.choice(['Retail', 'Agriculture', 'Manufacturing', 'Services'], num_samples),
    'total_credit_limit': np.random.uniform(2_000_000, 15_000_000, num_samples),
})

# 12 Months Prior to Default: Standard business utilizing ~40% of its lines
sme_data['utilization_12m_prior'] = np.random.uniform(0.20, 0.60, num_samples)
sme_data['balance_12m_prior'] = sme_data['total_credit_limit'] * sme_data['utilization_12m_prior']
sme_data['undrawn_commitment_12m_prior'] = sme_data['total_credit_limit'] - sme_data['balance_12m_prior']

# At Default: Distress causes a spike in drawings. High-risk sectors draw down heavily.
# We model that distressed SMEs draw down an additional 30% to 80% of their remaining limit.
additional_drawdown_pct = np.random.uniform(0.30, 0.80, num_samples)
sme_data['balance_at_default'] = sme_data['balance_12m_prior'] + (sme_data['undrawn_commitment_12m_prior'] * additional_drawdown_pct)

# Enforce a logical floor and ceiling (cannot draw past the absolute authorized limit)
sme_data['balance_at_default'] = np.minimum(sme_data['balance_at_default'], sme_data['total_credit_limit'])

# 2. Calculate the Empirical CCF for each defaulted historical facility
sme_data['empirical_ccf'] = (sme_data['balance_at_default'] - sme_data['balance_12m_prior']) / sme_data['undrawn_commitment_12m_prior']

# 3. Aggregate CCF by Sector to drive your risk-adjusted pricing matrix
sector_ccf_matrix = sme_data.groupby('sector')['empirical_ccf'].mean().reset_index()
sector_ccf_matrix.columns = ['SME Sector', 'Modeled Basel / IFRS 9 CCF']

print(sector_ccf_matrix.to_markdown(index=False, floatfmt=".2%"))

P = np.array([

import pandas as pd

def calculate_ifrs9_ecl(stage, principal, eir, lgd, remaining_years, annual_12m_pd, cumulative_lifetime_pd):
    """
    Computes IFRS 9 Compliant Expected Credit Loss (ECL)
    Stage 1: Uses 12-Month PD applied to current outstanding exposure.
    Stage 2: Models a dynamic multi-year cash flow drawdown decay over remaining life.
    """
    if stage == 1:
        # Simple 12-Month Horizon Calculation
        ecl = principal * annual_12m_pd * lgd
        calculation_path = f"12M PD ({annual_12m_pd*100:.2f}%) × LGD × Principal"
        return ecl, calculation_path
    
    elif stage == 2:
        # Construct multi-period marginal default buckets
        # For simulation, we distribute the cumulative lifetime PD linearly over remaining periods
        avg_marginal_pd = cumulative_lifetime_pd / remaining_years
        total_lifetime_ecl = 0
        
        for t in range(1, remaining_years + 1):
            # Model typical amortization: principal decreases, but EAD includes undrawn risk factors if revolving
            ead_t = principal * (1 - (0.15 * (t - 1))) # 15% structural principal reduction per year
            ead_t = max(ead_t, 0)
            
            # Discount loss back to Present Value using EIR
            discount_factor = (1 + eir) ** t
            marginal_loss = (avg_marginal_pd * lgd * ead_t) / discount_factor
            total_lifetime_ecl += marginal_loss
            
        calculation_path = f"Discounted cumulative cash-flow pool over {remaining_years} Years"
        return total_lifetime_ecl, calculation_path

🧮 The CVA Equation The CVA is calculated by discounting the expected positive exposure of the swap, multiplied by the counterparty's marginal probability of default and loss severity: \[\text{CVA}=(1-\text{R})\times \sum _{t=1}^{T}\text{BEE}_{t}\times \text{PD}_{t}\times \text{DF}_{t}\]\((1 - \text{R})\): The Loss Given Default (LGD) of the swap counterparty.\[\text{BEE}_{t}\] (Baseline Expected Exposure): The average positive market value (Mark-to-Market) of the swap contract during time bucket \[t\]. If the swap value goes negative, the bank owes the counterparty, meaning the bank faces zero credit risk from them at that moment.\[\text{PD}_{t}\]: The marginal probability that the swap counterparty defaults during period \[t\].\[\text{DF}_{t}\]: The risk-free discount factor used to bring future losses back to present value. 📈 Impact on the Syndicated RAROC Sheet The calculated CVA acts as a direct reduction to your day-one revenue lines. In large-scale project finance syndications, omitting the CVA overstates your initial deal yield: \[\text{Adjusted\ RAROC\ Numerator}=\text{Interest\ Yield}+\frac{\text{Upfront\ Arrangement\ Fees}-\textbf{Calculated\ Swap\ CVA}}{\text{Deal\ Tenor}}-\text{EL}-\text{Ops\ Costs}\]📊 Part 2: Basel III/IV Regulatory Capital vs. Economic Capital (IRB Framework)To maintain absolute capital discipline, credit committees must constantly compare Regulatory Capital (RC)—driven by rigid, compliance-based Basel rules—against Economic Capital (EC), which uses internal, risk-sensitive Value-at-Risk (VaR) models. 🧮 The Advanced Internal Ratings-Based (A-IRB) Capital Model Under Basel rules, the regulatory capital required for corporate and project exposures is derived using the official Basel Credit Risk Standard Formulation: \[\text{Correlation\ }(R)=0.12\times \left(\frac{1-e^{-50\times \text{PD}}}{1-e^{-50}}\right)+0.24\times \left[1-\left(\frac{1-e^{-50\times \text{PD}}}{1-e^{-50}}\right)\right]\]\[\text{Capital\ Requirement\ }(K)=\left[\text{LGD}\times N\left(\frac{G(\text{PD})+\sqrt{R}\times G(0.999)}{\sqrt{1-R}}\right)-(\text{PD}\times \text{LGD})\right]\times \text{Maturity\ Adjustment}\]\[\text{Regulatory\ Capital\ (RC)}=K\times \text{EAD}\times 8\%\]💻 Advanced Python Model: Dual-Engine Capital Framework (CVA + Basel IRB)This Python script builds an institutional credit risk engine. from scipy.optimize import brentq

# Bivariate Normal CDF approximation framework for Geske calculations
def bivariate_normal_cdf(a, b, rho):
    return stats.mvn.mvnun(np.array([-np.inf, -np.inf]), np.array([a, b]), np.array([0.0, 0.0]), np.array([[1.0, rho], [rho, 1.0]]))[0]

def calculate_geske_equity(V0, K1, K2, T1, T2, r, sigma_V):
    # 1. Numerically solve for the critical asset boundary (V*) at time T1
    # V* is the asset value where Equity(V*, T2-T1) == K1
    def equity_at_t1(V_test):
        d1 = (np.log(V_test / K2) + (r + 0.5 * sigma_V**2) * (T2 - T1)) / (sigma_V * np.sqrt(T2 - T1))
        d2 = d1 - sigma_V * np.sqrt(T2 - T1)
        eq_val = V_test * stats.norm.cdf(d1) - K2 * np.exp(-r * (T2 - T1)) * stats.norm.cdf(d2)
        return eq_val - K1

    # Solve for V* boundary line
    V_star = brentq(equity_at_t1, 1e-5, V0 * 5)
    
    # 2. Compute standardized pricing parameters
    rho = np.sqrt(T1 / T2)
    
    a1 = (np.log(V0 / V_star) + (r + 0.5 * sigma_V**2) * T1) / (sigma_V * np.sqrt(T1))
    b1 = a1 - sigma_V * np.sqrt(T1)
    
    a2 = (np.log(V0 / K2) + (r + 0.5 * sigma_V**2) * T2) / (sigma_V * np.sqrt(T2))
    b2 = a2 - sigma_V * np.sqrt(T2)
    
    # 3. Aggregate joint probabilities across both tranches
    m1 = bivariate_normal_cdf(a1, a2, rho)
    m2 = bivariate_normal_cdf(b1, b2, rho)
    
    equity_value = V0 * m1 - K2 * np.exp(-r * T2) * m2 - K1 * np.exp(-r * T1) * stats.norm.pdf(b1)
    return max(equity_value, 0)

# --- Standard Black-Scholes Engine to extract Market Implied Volatility ---
def black_scholes_call(S, K, T, r, sigma):
    d1 = (np.log(S / K) + (r + 0.5 * sigma**2) * T) / (sigma * np.sqrt(T))
    d2 = d1 - sigma * np.sqrt(T)
    return S * stats.norm.cdf(d1) - K * np.exp(-r * T) * stats.norm.cdf(d2)

def implied_volatility_inversion(target_price, S, K, T, r):
    def objective(sig):
        return black_scholes_call(S, K, T, r, sig) - target_price
    try:
        return brentq(objective, 1e-4, 3.0)
    except:
        return np.nan

It uses data pseudonymization to process a corporate loan through your Basel IRB and RAROC systems without exposing sensitive corporate identities. pythonimport uuid
import hashlib
import numpy as np
import scipy.stats as stats

class SecureCorporateCreditEngine:
    """
    Enterprise-grade SaaS computation engine implementing privacy-preserving 
    portfolio steering calculations.
    """
    def __init__(self, tenant_id: str):
        self.tenant_id = tenant_id # Enforces multi-tenant logical routing
        
    def anonymize_corporate_identity(self, raw_company_name: str) -> str:
        """
        Creates a deterministic SHA-256 pseudonymous token from the client name.
        Ensures PII never crosses into the calculation database logs.
        """
        salted_input = f"{raw_company_name}_{self.tenant_id}"
        return hashlib.sha256(salted_input.encode()).hexdigest()[:16]

    def compute_secure_irb_raroc(self, ead: float, pd: float, lgd: float, 
                                 interest_rate: float, cost_of_funds: float, 
                                 maturity_years: int) -> dict:
        # Basel III/IV Asset Correlation Profile Formula
        R = 0.12 * ((1 - np.exp(-50 * pd)) / (1 - np.exp(-50))) + \
            0.24 * (1 - ((1 - np.exp(-50 * pd)) / (1 - np.exp(-50))))
        
        # Maturity Factor Adjustment derivation
        b = (0.11852 - 0.05478 * np.log(pd)) ** 2
        maturity_adj = (1 + (maturity_years - 2.5) * b) / (1 - 1.5 * b)
        
        # Compute capital requirement factor (K)
        g_pd = stats.norm.ppf(pd)
        g_target = stats.norm.ppf(0.999) # 99.9% Regulatory Capital Confidence Bound
        K = (lgd * stats.norm.cdf((g_pd + np.sqrt(R) * g_target) / np.sqrt(1 - R)) - (pd * lgd)) * maturity_adj
        
        regulatory_capital = K * ead * 0.08
        economic_capital = ead * 0.095 # Internal Bank Value-at-Risk Capital Allocation
        
        # Compute Yield metrics
        expected_loss = ead * pd * lgd
        net_margin = (ead * interest_rate) - (ead * cost_of_funds) - expected_loss
        raroc = (net_margin / economic_capital) * 100
        
        return {
            "Basel_Regulatory_Capital": round(regulatory_capital, 2),
            "Internal_Economic_Capital": round(economic_capital, 2),
            "Expected_Loss_Impairment": round(expected_loss, 2),
            "Calculated_Asset_RAROC_Pct": round(raroc, 2)
        }
